How to clean your Glock – a step-by-step cleaning guide
Renato Brodar Renato Brodar27. May, 2025
Whether you’re a seasoned gun owner or a new Glock enthusiast, regular cleaning is crucial for maintaining performance, safety, and longevity. Glocks are known for their reliability and ease of maintenance, but even the most dependable firearm needs proper care. In this guide, we’ll walk you through a simple, step-by-step process to clean your Glock safely and effectively. From disassembly to lubrication, you’ll learn exactly what you need to keep your handgun in top condition after every trip to the range or day in the field. Let’s get started.

Step-by-Step Process

Before you begin, make sure you’re working in a clean, well-lit area with the necessary supplies: a cleaning mat, solvent, lubricant, cleaning rod or bore brush, patches, and a nylon brush. Most importantly, always ensure your Glock is unloaded before handling or disassembling it. Start by removing the magazine and checking the chamber twice. Once you’re sure the firearm is safe, proceed with field stripping: separate the slide, barrel, and recoil spring assembly from the frame. With these components apart, you can clean each section thoroughly—starting with the barrel, then the slide, followed by the frame. Use solvent to remove carbon buildup, scrub with your brush, and wipe everything clean with a dry cloth. If you are using aggressive solvent – like Ballistol Robla Solo Force – be careful with it (do not leave it for too long and make sure you really remove it after). I am more of a fan of gentle solvents – like FlunaTec Gun Cleaner etc. Finally, apply a light coat of lubricant to key contact points before reassembling your Glock. This simple routine keeps your firearm functioning smoothly and safely.


1. Ensure the Glock Is Unloaded

Safety first: Remove the magazine by pressing the magazine release.
Check the chamber: Pull the slide back and lock it open. Visually and physically inspect the chamber to confirm it’s empty.
Dry fire: Point the firearm in a safe direction and pull the trigger to release the striker. This is necessary for disassembly.

2. Field Strip the Glock

Pull the slide slightly back (about a quarter inch).
Pull down the takedown levers (both sides simultaneously).
Let the slide move forward and off the frame.
Remove the recoil spring assembly by lifting it out of the slide.
Lift out the barrel from the slide.

3. Clean the Barrel

Apply solvent to a bore brush or patch and run it through the barrel several times.
Use a dry patch to remove loosened debris and excess solvent.
Repeat until the patches come out clean. You can also help yourself with a cleaning brush.
Inspect the barrel for any remaining residue or obstructions.

4. Clean the Slide

Wipe down the interior with a cloth and solvent.
Use a nylon brush to scrub areas like the slide rails, breech face, and extractor. For me it is not unusual to use ear sticks for cleaning slide rails…
Dry thoroughly with a clean cloth or patch.

5. Clean the Frame

Use a dry brush or cloth to remove debris from the frame, especially around the trigger mechanism and rails.
Avoid over-saturating with solvent, as it can affect internal components.
6. Lubricate Key Parts

Apply a light coat of oil to the following areas:

Slide Rails (4 points)
Apply a thin film of oil on each of the four frame rails (where the slide rides on the frame).
Do the same inside the slide where it contacts the rails.
Barrel Exterior
Lightly oil the outside of the barrel, especially around the chamber area where it contacts the slide.
Barrel Lug
Put a drop of oil on the barrel lug (the rounded part that locks into the frame) where it meets the slide lock.
Inside the Slide (Where the Trigger Bar Contacts)
Apply a drop of oil on the connector where the trigger bar and connector meet inside the slide.
Recoil Spring Assembly (Optional)
Wipe it down, but it doesn’t typically need oil unless it looks dry.

DO NOT:

Over-lubricate. Glocks don’t need much oil—too much can attract carbon and dirt.
Oil the firing pin channel or the inside of the slide excessively. This can interfere with proper function.
7. Reassemble the Glock

Insert the barrel back into the slide.
Replace the recoil spring assembly, seating it against the barrel lug.
Slide the upper back onto the frame until it clicks into place.

8. Function Check

Rack the slide and dry fire in a safe direction to ensure proper function.
Check reset by holding the trigger down, racking the slide again, and releasing the trigger slowly to hear the click.
Following this routine regularly will keep your Glock running reliably and ready for use. Clean after every shooting session or at least once every few months if stored.

Properly cleaning your Glock pistol not only ensures its reliability and performance but also extends its lifespan. By following a consistent maintenance routine, you’re investing in your firearm’s safety and accuracy. With just a little time and care, your Glock will be ready whenever you need it—clean, smooth, and dependable.
